## Curriculum policy

This hub is an original, bilingual, visual-learning companion. Use current official curriculum documents as the authority for scope; write original explanations, examples, diagrams, animations, questions and code. Do not reproduce NCERT textbook prose, textbook diagrams, or page layouts.

**Source hierarchy**
1. Current CBSE curriculum for the relevant academic year and subject code, where the course is a CBSE elective.
2. Official NCERT textbook chapters and learning sequence where NCERT publishes a relevant textbook.

4. Bihar SCERT/BSEB syllabus when the hub is specifically used for a Bihar-board examination; mark any differences explicitly.

## Curriculum tracks and scope

### Class VIII — Computational Thinking and AI foundation

There is no separate NCERT Class VIII Computer Science textbook sequence identified as the authority for this hub. Build a **framework-aligned foundation course**, not a fictitious NCERT chapter list. Confirm the precise module outcomes against the current school/CBSE CT & AI framework before tagging them as prescribed.

Proposed original learning modules:
1. **Patterns, Data and Computational Thinking** — recognise patterns, represent information and reason with data.
2. **Decomposition, Algorithms and Debugging** — break problems into steps, trace instructions and correct errors.
3. **Digital Systems and How Computers Represent Information** — inputs, processes, outputs, files and simple data representation.
4. **AI Around Us: Data, Models and Bias** — understand AI through simple, safe demonstrations; distinguish data, rules, predictions and bias.
5. **Networks, Privacy and Digital Citizenship** — understand basic connectivity, personal data, safe choices and respectful participation.
6. **Design Challenge: Solve a Real Problem** — use computational thinking to design and explain a small solution.

These are proposed hub module titles, **not official NCERT chapter names**. Remove or re-scope any item that is not supported by the current framework or school syllabus.

### Class IX — NCERT ICT sequence, cross-checked against CBSE Computer Applications (165)

Use the eight chapters in the official NCERT Class IX ICT textbook as the chapter structure. The 2026–27 CBSE Computer Applications syllabus is a separate current subject curriculum; add cross-reference labels for its Basics of Information Technology, Cyber Safety, Office Tools and practical outcomes. Do not imply that every NCERT ICT chapter is a separately assessed unit in Computer Applications.

Canonical chapter sequence:
1. **Introduction to ICT** — ICT in daily life; computer-system concepts and digital tools as supporting concepts, without expanding into a duplicate standalone hardware chapter.
2. **Creating Textual Communication** — create, format, review and share text documents.
3. **Creating Visual Communication** — images, visual composition, diagrams and charts.
4. **Creating Audio–Video Communication** — plan, record, edit, caption and responsibly share media.
5. **Presenting Ideas** — organise information into clear, accessible presentations and animate meaningfully.
6. **Getting Connected: Internet** — internet, web, browsers, services and safe information search.
7. **Safety and Security in the Cyber World** — privacy, security threats, safe practices and responses.
8. **Fun with Logic** — logic, patterns, reasoning and introductory computational problem-solving.

**Consolidation rule:** existing Class IX pages on hardware/memory, software/OS, files/folders, bits/bytes and algorithms/flowcharts are not allowed to become extra competing chapters. Reuse their accurate teaching material as relevant subsections, labs or interactive demonstrations inside the canonical eight chapters, or mark genuinely advanced content as optional enrichment. Each concept has one canonical home and may link to it from other chapters rather than repeat its full lesson.

### Class X — CBSE Computer Applications (165), 2026–27

Organise content around the current official units and practical work:
1. **Networking and Internet Services** — WWW, clients/servers, URLs, protocols, web services and mobile technologies. This advances Class IX internet literacy; it must not repeat the introductory safety lesson.
2. **HTML: Build a Web Page** — document structure, semantic elements, text, images, lists, tables, links, forms, audio/video and accessible alternatives.
3. **CSS and Page Design** — styling, layout, spacing, typography, colour and responsive presentation, aligned to the prescribed scope.
4. **Cyber Ethics and Responsible Web Publishing** — netiquette, open-source software, intellectual property, plagiarism, privacy, fraud and responsible publishing.
5. **Integrated Practical: Build and Explain a Small Website** — a practical/project space that combines the official skills, not a new theory unit.

### Class XI — Students choose one senior-secondary track

Computer Science (083) and Informatics Practices (065) are **different CBSE subjects**. Do not combine them into one syllabus. The home page must clearly distinguish the two tracks; shared prerequisite concepts may be linked, not duplicated as competing chapters.

#### Track A — Computer Science (083), Class XI
1. **Computer Systems and Organisation** — hardware/software, CPU, memory, OS, translators, Boolean logic, number systems and encoding schemes.
2. **Problem Solving, Algorithms and Decomposition** — analyse a problem, algorithm, flowchart, pseudocode, testing and debugging.
3. **Python Foundations** — modes, tokens, variables, data types, operators, expressions, conversions, input/output and errors.
4. **Control Flow and Iteration** — conditions, loops, tracing and nested loops.
5. **Strings and Collections** — strings, lists, tuples and dictionaries, their operations and algorithms.
6. **Python Modules and Applied Problem Solving** — prescribed math/random/statistics module use and practice.
7. **Society, Law and Ethics** — digital footprints, netiquette, IPR/licensing, cyber safety, malware, e-waste, IT Act, inclusion.

#### Track B — Informatics Practices (065), Class XI
1. **Introduction to Computer Systems** — evolution, components, memory, data recovery/security and software types.
2. **Python: Core Concepts and Control Flow** — syntax, data types, operators, input/output, conversion, debugging and conditions/loops.
3. **Lists and Dictionaries** — creation, traversal, mutation, methods and data-oriented problem solving.
4. **Introduction to NumPy** — create and inspect arrays from lists as prescribed.
5. **Database Concepts and Relational Data** — DBMS, relations, attributes, tuples, domains and keys.
6. **SQL Foundations with MySQL** — DDL/DML, table creation, data types, SELECT/WHERE, filters and row modification.
7. **Emerging Trends in Computing** — AI/ML/NLP, AR/VR, robotics, big data, IoT/sensors, cloud, grid and blockchain, with careful distinctions between established and emerging technologies.

### Class XII — Continue the selected Class XI track

#### Track A — Computer Science (083), Class XII
1. **Functions, Parameters and Scope** — built-in/module/user-defined functions, arguments, return values and execution flow.
2. **Exception Handling** — try/except/finally and resilient program behaviour.
3. **File Handling** — text, binary/pickle and CSV files; paths, modes, reading, writing, searching, updating and appending.
4. **Stacks** — push/pop and stack implementation using lists.
5. **Computer Networks and Web Services** — data communication, bandwidth, IP addressing, switching, media, devices, topologies, protocols, URLs, hosting and web services.
6. **Database Management and SQL** — relational model, keys, SQL types/constraints, DDL/DML, queries, aggregates, grouping, joins and NULL handling.
7. **Python–SQL Connectivity and Integrated Project** — connections, cursors, parameterised queries, transactions and a useful project.

#### Track B — Informatics Practices (065), Class XII
1. **Pandas Series** — create, select, index, slice and transform Series.
2. **Pandas DataFrames and CSV Data** — create, inspect, iterate, filter, update, rename, delete, import and export.
3. **Data Visualisation with Matplotlib** — line plots, bar graphs, histograms, labels, titles and legends.
4. **SQL Beyond the Basics** — mathematical, text and date functions; aggregates; GROUP BY, HAVING, ORDER BY and joins.
5. **Computer Networks and Web Concepts** — PAN/LAN/MAN/WAN, devices, topologies, URLs, WWW, web browsers, websites, hosting and cookies.
6. **Societal Impacts of Technology** — digital footprint, netiquette, data protection, IPR, plagiarism, FOSS/licensing, cybercrime, IT Act, e-waste and health.
7. **Data Project and Practical Portfolio** — analyse a real, safe dataset, visualise findings and document an original application.

## No-repetition and progression rules

- Give each concept one canonical chapter. Cross-links and short prerequisite refreshers are allowed; repeating a full lesson is not.
- Revisit a concept only when the next class adds a clearly identified new depth or skill. State what is new.
- Do not teach Class XI/XII Computer Science and Informatics Practices as if they were interchangeable.
- Keep syllabus-required content distinct from optional enrichment and project labs.
- For every chapter, record official source, subject code, academic year, covered outcomes, prerequisite links, and visual interaction plan.
- Do not claim "NCERT prescribed" for a topic supported only by a general framework, older syllabus or enrichment plan.

## Chapter design and visual-learning acceptance standard

Class IX Chapter 1 is the visual design benchmark for the site’s shared layout, but **not** the content template. Every chapter must use the shared CSS/JS and preserve consistent typography, spacing, controls, bilingual formatting, responsive behaviour, notes, print and accessibility.

Every substantial concept should be taught visually:
1. Short, clear English/Hindi explanation with matching semantic formatting.
2. Original labelled diagram, stateful animation, flow or visual model suited to the concept.
3. Learner-controlled interaction (tap/click, step, toggle, reset, trace, or safe custom input).
4. A worked example or trace when it improves understanding; do not force an example into every subsection.
5. Immediate, meaningful feedback and a short check for understanding.
6. Study & Copy notes, exam-style answers where relevant, and an appropriate practical activity.

A chapter is not complete merely because it contains text, decorative animation, or a single diagram. Its key outcomes must have meaningful visual explanations and working interactions. Mobile touch interaction is required; do not rely on hover. Respect reduced-motion preferences without hiding content or making learning impossible. Provide accessible labels and non-animated explanations of all essential visual information.
