# Class IX curriculum blueprint — 2026–27

**Status:** working teaching blueprint; not a claim of BSEB/SCERT prescription or of complete NCERT/CBSE assessment compliance.

## 1. Source and scope

### Primary learning sequence

Use the eight-chapter NCERT *Information and Communication Technology* textbook for Class IX, reprint 2025–26, as the chapter sequence. The official NCERT preface describes four learning strands: **Connecting with the World; Connecting with Each Other; Creating with ICT; Interacting with ICT**. Keep the eight chapter names and order intact. The hub's outcomes and activities below are original teaching interpretations, not verbatim NCERT learning outcomes.

Official source: https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php?iict1=ps-8

### Separate 2026–27 CT & AI requirement (CBSE-specific)

CBSE Circular Acad-18/2026 (9 April 2026) says NCERT is to provide CT&AI modules for Classes IX–XII for 2026–27, to be transacted as modules for internal assessment. The circular also says other AI courses run through 2025–26 are discontinued for Class IX in 2026–27. This is not a ninth ICT textbook chapter and must not be merged into the eight-chapter sequence.

## 2. Course-level outcomes

By the end of the eight-chapter ICT sequence, a learner should be able to:

1. Explain how ICT supports learning, communication, information access and everyday services, while checking the reliability and appropriateness of information.
2. Create and revise a structured text document for a real audience, then save and share it appropriately.
3. Select, compose and interpret visual information (images, diagrams and charts) without misleading the audience.
4. Plan and produce a simple audio/video message, considering recording, editing, captions, accessibility, consent and responsible sharing.
5. Organise a short presentation around a clear message, readable slides and an effective spoken explanation.
6. Distinguish the Internet from the Web and describe, at an age-appropriate level, how a browser request reaches a server and returns a response.
7. Recognise common cyber risks and select safe, privacy-preserving responses, including seeking help and reporting incidents.
8. Use patterns, logical rules, algorithms and flowcharts to describe and trace simple problem-solving processes.
9. Transfer these skills to a small, documented digital communication task, explaining choices and checking the quality of the result.

## 4. Recommended learning progression

1. **Understand the digital world (Ch. 1):** establish vocabulary, system thinking and data/information foundations.
2. **Create clear messages (Ch. 2–5):** move from text to visual, audio/video and slide-based communication. Reuse one consistent theme (for example, a school science event) so learners practise a connected production workflow rather than four unrelated definitions.
3. **Connect and stay safe (Ch. 6–7):** understand the delivery channel before learning how to protect oneself and others while using it.
4. **Reason and model (Ch. 8):** consolidate structured thinking through algorithms and flowcharts. Link back to the planning and troubleshooting already practised in document/media tasks.

Prerequisite rule: introduce a term before using it in an assessment. Revisit prior ideas briefly when needed, but do not duplicate full chapters.

## 5. Cross-chapter project: communicate a school activity

Learners create a small, coherent communication package for a fictional or teacher-approved school activity:

- write a factual notice (Ch. 2);
- create a labelled poster or simple chart (Ch. 3);
- plan a short captioned audio/video announcement (Ch. 4);
- arrange a concise slide presentation (Ch. 5);
- explain how it could be shared safely online (Ch. 6–7);
- show the workflow as an ordered algorithm or flowchart (Ch. 8).

Use fictional names/contact details in public demonstrations. Do not upload students' personal information, faces, voices or school records to public tools without the required consent and safeguards. A low-bandwidth/offline alternative must be available for every core activity.

## 6. Assessment design (hub recommendation, not official weightage)

Use a balanced evidence model; do not publish these proportions as CBSE/BSEB marks:

- **Explain:** short oral/written answers that check understanding, not memorised labels.
- **Demonstrate:** trace a process, identify a component, interpret a visual or choose a safe response.
- **Create:** produce a document, visual, media plan or slide sequence against a short rubric.
- **Reflect:** explain one design decision, one correction and one safety/accessibility check.

Suggested per-chapter checks: a small set of recall/understanding questions, at least one application question, one scenario or worked trace, and one practical artefact or interaction. Provide model answers only after learners attempt the question. Accept equivalent correct reasoning and do not reward decorative formatting over accuracy.

### Common practical rubric

| Criterion | Evidence of success |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Facts, values, labels and instructions are correct. |
| Purpose and audience | The artefact answers a real communication need for its intended reader/viewer. |
| Structure and clarity | Information is ordered, readable and complete. |
| Tool/process understanding | The learner can explain the steps and make a purposeful choice. |
| Safety and responsibility | Privacy, consent, attribution and safe sharing are considered where relevant. |
| Reflection and correction | The learner can identify and improve at least one weakness. |

## 7. Content and interaction quality gates

A chapter may be marked **content-ready** only when all relevant items are present:

- [ ] Every key outcome has a clear beginner-first explanation in English and matching Hindi.
- [ ] Technical vocabulary is defined before use; examples are accurate and age-appropriate.
- [ ] At least one worked example or scenario makes abstract concepts concrete.
- [ ] A topic-specific diagram, trace or visual model explains the actual concept (not decorative animation).
- [ ] The learner can perform a meaningful action and see a result; every control works.
- [ ] There is a non-drag alternative, keyboard access, visible focus, accessible feedback and reduced-motion behavior.
- [ ] Study & Copy contains connected explanatory theory, not merely a duplicate question bank.
- [ ] Questions include understanding, application and reasoning; model answers are accurate and bilingual.
- [ ] Practical activities have an offline/low-bandwidth path and avoid real personal data.
- [ ] Navigation, language toggle, copy, print and links pass browser checks on desktop and mobile.
- [ ] The official source, version, coverage and any local-board differences are recorded.

Track each chapter separately as **mapped**, **content-ready**, **interaction-tested**, and **live-verified**. These are different states; do not collapse them into one "complete" label.
